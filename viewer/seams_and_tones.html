Seams and Tones

A mother seam turns counterclockwise by a, a father seam clockwise by b. Every generation, every counterclockwise seam meets every clockwise seam, and each meeting has a child carrying the difference. Parents keep sounding. The family becomes neutral when a child is born at exactly zero: a seam has met its exact opposite.

The fabric

Across: once around the child. Down: generations. Each seam present winds the phase by its own amount; hue is the combined phase, and the dark threads are seams where the waves cancel. Below the green line the family has a neutral child.

The tones

Each seam is a tone: pitch from its size, counterclockwise and clockwise half a cycle apart. A meeting of two tones sounds their difference, the child. When a tone meets its exact opposite, they cancel to silence.

What the lineage shows

  • Neutral exactly when the ratio is a fraction. Seams built from a and b can only meet their exact opposite if a/b is a ratio of whole numbers. Otherwise they come closer and closer forever and never cancel. Try the golden ratio or √2.
  • Every pairing at once is much faster than one path. Subtracting the smaller from the larger along a single line can take as many generations as the denominator. With every pairing present, comparable parents find their opposite in about log₂ of the denominator.
  • Lopsided parents are still slow. If one parent is a twelfth of the other, it takes about twelve generations whatever else happens: the weak parent can only chip away.
  • Primes don't show up here. Prime and composite denominators of the same size resolve in the same number of generations, on average to within 0.02 of a generation.
  • The slider is always rational. Its steps are thousandths, so slider values always resolve eventually. The irrational presets are the ones that never do.
