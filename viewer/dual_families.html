Dual Families

Two sets of horn tori, A and B, the dual tetrahedra generalized. A torus with k children has k parents. Positive charges arrive at one end of its axis (the mother's side) and negative charges at the other (the father's side). Every parent comes from the other set. With these rules the smallest family has 4k tori, and the page shows one for each k.

Why the smallest family has 4k tori

  • A torus with k children has k parents, and no torus is the child of its own child, so it is linked to 2k different tori.
  • All of them are in the other set, so each set needs at least 2k tori, and the family needs at least 4k.
  • The family shown reaches that bound for every k. Every torus is linked to every torus of the other set: k as its children and k as its parents.
  • For k = 2 this is your dual tetrahedron: 8 tori at the corners of a cube, each linked to the four corners of the other tetrahedron.

Charges

  • Each torus sends one large charge and k − 1 small ones, adding to zero: (+1, −1) for k = 2; (+2/3, −1/3, −1/3) for k = 3; (+1, +1, −1, −1) for k = 4; (+4/5, −1/5 four times) for k = 5.
  • Each torus also receives exactly the same pattern, so arrivals balance too. The charge pattern passes unchanged around the family.
