Lineage Disk

An infinite, negatively curved space drawn inside a finite circle. Its edge is infinitely far away, yet you can see all of it. The space is tiled by triangles whose corners sit on the edge at the fractions. Each triangle is a family: two synchronous parents and the child born between them. Every relation is a direction from the centre. Head that way and you pass through generation after generation of families. A fraction ends in its own resting circle; an irrational never arrives.

Tap any triangle to see its family. Fractions sit around the edge: 0 at the left, 1 at the top, ∞ at the right, and negatives below. The green discs are resting circles, one per fraction, smaller for larger denominators.

How the rules sit in this space

  • Room to branch. The space grows exponentially outward, as fast as a lineage that keeps branching. In flat space the branches would crowd together.
  • Every triangle is a family. Its two parents p/q and r/s are synchronous: p·s − q·r = ±1, so their clocks line up. The child born between them is (p + r)/(q + s): counts add.
  • Depth is scale. Each step toward the edge is one generation, a finer layer. The edge itself is infinitely far: a journey there is endless inside, yet the whole thing fits in the circle you see. That's an aeon inside an instant.
  • A direction is a relation. The angle you set out at from the centre decides where you land on the edge. A fraction ends inside its own resting circle after finitely many generations: the neutral child. An irrational slips between resting circles forever.
  • The path here is the single line of descent. It's the subtract-the-smaller path (Euclid's algorithm). The every-pairing-at-once lineage from earlier reaches neutrality faster, by shortcuts this picture doesn't draw.
