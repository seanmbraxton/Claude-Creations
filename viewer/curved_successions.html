Curved Successions

The sphere is tiled by curved triangles whose corners are 90°, 60° and 360°/n. Where n of them meet, one succession is a turn of 360°/n. After n successions the parent is back, so it is its own child n generations down. On a flat surface those angles add to exactly 180° only when n = 6. For n = 3 or 4 they add to more, which is why these live on a sphere.

Drag to turn it. The gold arrow is the parent, and the faint arrows are where its descendants land. The shaded and plain triangles alternate, and the turning point sits on the axis drawn through the sphere.

When does succession close?

nAngle sumSpace

Counting that wraps around quickly needs a sphere. At n = 6 the space is flat (the 60°/120° torus). Beyond that it is saddle-shaped, and when succession never closes you get the ordinary integers.
