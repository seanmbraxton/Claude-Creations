Seam Passage

Each torus, opened flat, carries a mother seam (+) and a father seam (−): open lines of width ε, each ending in a pair of vortices. Where they overlap their mismatches combine, and that overlap is the child's seam, with new vortices born at its ends. A traveller comes toward the child's seam at the angle you set. Steep, it jumps the gap in an instant. Grazing, it climbs the walls into the child, where the seam looks wider and the angle steeper, and so on down until it can cross or reaches the neutral child.

The ladder

The observer's holes

Every climb appends a branch: a whole torus that the observer, inside, sees only as one more hole. Every jump across adds nothing.

The rules in play

  • Seams are open lines with vortex ends. Each seam runs from a counterclockwise vortex to a clockwise one, so each torus is twisted locally but untwisted as a whole.
  • Overlap makes the child. The mother seam (+) and father seam (−) overlap, their mismatches combine, and the overlap carries the child's charge. The ends of the overlap are newborn vortices.
  • The angle decides. Time spent in a seam of width ε at angle θ is about ε/sin θ. Steep, that's a vanishing instant: you jump the gap. Within about ε of grazing you can't get across: you climb the walls.
  • Each level steepens the angle. Zooming into a seam of width ε stretches the across-direction by 1/ε, so the child sees tan θ′ = tan θ / ε. Flatter approaches climb more levels, about log(1/θ) / log(1/ε) of them.
  • The neutral child ends the climb. It has no seam. For rational parents the ladder has a bottom; for irrational ones it doesn't.
  • Instant above, aeon below. On the parent's clock a passage takes at most about one seam width, ε: a crossing takes ε/sin θ, and a climber simply vanishes into the seam. Each level down runs on a clock 1/ε times faster, so that same instant is lived as ε−depth times as long at the bottom.
