Twist Resolution

Each torus re-emits what its parents gave it. The mother's share (+, counterclockwise) goes to its counterclockwise children and the father's share (−, clockwise) to its clockwise children, split by the scalings below. Its local twist is mother plus father. One torus starts twisted +2/3 and another −2/3, so the family as a whole is untwisted. Watch whether the twist resolves as it passes down through the children.

What to look for

  • Resolution. With charge conserved (total scaling 1) and at least two children of each sign, the local twist spreads until every torus is untwisted, but only if the step between children shares no factor with the number of tori around the ring.
  • Trapped twist. If the step shares a factor with the ring size, the children only ever reach a sub-ring, the twist can't spread to the rest, and it never resolves. On a prime ring every step works.
  • Conservation is the knife edge. Below 1 total scaling, charge leaks away; above 1, it grows without limit.
  • Loop length and shear don't matter. Switching between 3 and 4 generations, or adding shear, only relabels which torus is which. The outcome depends only on the steps and the ring size.
  • Global twist never changes. It starts at zero and stays there. Only local twist moves around.
