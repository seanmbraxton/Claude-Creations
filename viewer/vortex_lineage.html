Vortex Lineage

Four horn tori sit at the corners of a tetrahedron. Each carries a balanced pair at two separate points on its surface: a +1 vortex on top of the tube and a −1 antivortex underneath. Each spike leaves straight out along the surface normal, curves outward, and arrives along the child's axis into its horn point. The +1 goes forward around the loop and the −1 backward, and both close after four generations.

What this shows

  • Every torus is balanced on its own. Its surface holds one vortex and one antivortex at two separate points, so the charges add to zero without the pair cancelling. Each spike leaves along the surface normal at its own vortex.
  • The −1 spikes trace the same family backward. Following the +1 spikes you go parent → child → grandchild → great-grandchild → parent. Following the −1 spikes from the parent you go parent → great-grandchild → grandchild → child → parent. The opposite charge visits the same lineage in reverse order.
  • Arrival is along the child's axis. Each child's axis lies along the direction of the loop. The +1 comes in traveling forward along it and the −1 traveling backward, so they enter the horn point through its two opposite cones.
  • The angles. From the center, any two corners are 109.47° apart. The readouts measure how closely each spike leaves along its surface normal and arrives along its child's axis.
