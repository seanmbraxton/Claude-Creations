Vortex Lineage

Four horn tori sit at the corners of a tetrahedron. Each carries a balanced pair: a +1 vortex and a −1 antivortex. The +1 spike curves forward into the next torus's horn point. The −1 spike does the same in the opposite direction, so each horn point receives one of each, through its two sides. Both loops close after four generations.

What this shows

  • Every torus is balanced on its own. Its surface holds one vortex and one antivortex, so the charges add to zero, as a torus requires. Its horn point also receives one of each.
  • The −1 spikes trace the same family backward. Following the +1 spikes you go parent → child → grandchild → great-grandchild → parent. Following the −1 spikes from the parent you go parent → great-grandchild → grandchild → child → parent. The opposite charge visits the same lineage in reverse order.
  • Each charge has its own side of the horn. A horn point is two cones meeting tip to tip. The +1 spike enters through one cone and the −1 spike through the other.
  • The angles. From the center, two corners are 109.47° apart. At a corner, two edges meet at 60° when the spikes are straight and at 120° when they bow out onto the sphere.
