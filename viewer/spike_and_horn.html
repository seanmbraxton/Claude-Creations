Spike and Horn

A path winds on the child horn torus and passes through its horn point once per poloidal turn. Each pass arrives from some direction around the axis: its entry angle. Spikes from the parent torus meet the horn point at fixed orientations. When a pass arrives lined up with a spike, it slides down that spike to the parent surface. Otherwise it stays on the child.

What this shows

  • Locked winding gives a few fixed entry angles. When poloidal turns are a fixed fraction p/t of toroidal turns, the passes arrive from only a handful of directions, set by that fraction. Line a spike up with one of them and those passes go down to the parent every time, at a steady rhythm.
  • Free winding spreads the entry angles all the way around. With golden or √2 − 1, no direction repeats. Passes only come near a spike now and then, and with zero tolerance none ever enters exactly.
  • This is the portal gate from the original brief, made geometric. The spike orientations are the windows. Zero tolerance is the horn case, ε = 0, where only exact alignment passes. A wider tolerance lets near-misses through, the way a nonzero ε did.
  • Descending needs locking. A path reaches the parent level only when its two windings are tied together. Free windings keep it on the child. On the tick staircase, the plateaus are the locked ratios, so the plateaus are the ratios that can reach the level below.
