Split Interference

A twisted torus holds an imbalance of M units. It splits that among k children every possible way at once. Each child sits where its vortex is on the parent's surface and carries a phase: its charge times its angle. All the splits are added together. Most cancel. The ones left over are the stable forms, and nobody chose them.

What the grid shows

  • With children evenly placed and one phase turn per unit, the survivors are exactly the divisors. M units split among k children survive only when k divides M, and then the sum is exactly 1 in size. Every other number of children cancels to exactly zero.
  • Primes are the charges with no stable middle. A prime imbalance survives only whole (one child) or as all ones (M children). Every split in between cancels itself out.
  • The rule needs complete windings. Slide the phase turns off a whole number and the cancellation goes ragged. The clean divisor pattern appears only when each unit of charge winds the phase all the way round.
  • Arcs sized by charge never cancel. When each child's arc matches its charge, every split carries the same phase, so they all add and nothing is selected.
